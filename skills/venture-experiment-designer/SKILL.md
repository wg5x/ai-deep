# Venture Experiment Designer

## Core Rule

Every opportunity must become an experiment before it becomes a belief.

Use small experiments for cash-flow speed. Use large experiments for long-term upside. Keep both in the portfolio.

## Experiment Types

| Type | Window | Goal | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Small | 1-7 days | Find fast cash or rejection | Replies, trial orders, small payments |
| Medium | 2-8 weeks | Test repeatable service | Repeat customers, referrals, delivery templates |
| Large | 3-18 months | Test major venture thesis | Retention, scalable demand, strategic advantage |

Default early-stage allocation:

```text
70% small cash-flow experiments
20% medium repeatability experiments
10% large venture options
```

## Design Checklist

```text
Opportunity:
Experiment size:
Target buyer:
Pain or desire:
Why now:
Leverage available:
Small-boat test:
Offer:
Price or value exchange:
Delivery method:
AI leverage:
Acquisition path:
7-day actions:
Success threshold:
Failure threshold:
Risks:
What will be learned:
```

## Rules

- Small experiments must be sellable within 7 days.
- Medium experiments must test repeatability, not just one lucky sale.
- Large experiments need milestones; do not judge them only by week-one revenue.
- Do not build software before proving a buyer, unless the software itself is the cheapest proof.
- If no buyer can be named, downgrade to research.
- Prefer borrowed tools, existing platforms, manual service, and AI-assisted delivery before custom systems.
- If failure is expensive or hard to reverse, shrink the experiment before executing.

## Example Framing

Bad:

```text
Build an AI platform for merchants.
```

Good:

```text
Offer 10 Beijing restaurants a World Cup 7-day viewing campaign pack for 499-999 RMB.
Measure replies, payments, and reusable delivery steps.
```

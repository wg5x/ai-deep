## 2. Strategic Implication

The first validation market should be China-first, not English-first.

This is not only a translation decision. It changes the operating model.

Instead of assuming a Western SaaS path:

```text
landing page
→ email list
→ cold outreach
→ subscription
```

the first Chinese-market path should assume:

```text
content platform exposure
→ comments/private messages
→ WeChat follow-up
→ trust through conversation and artifacts
→ small paid service or customized brief
→ later productization
```

The initial operating loop should be:

```text
Xiaohongshu/Bilibili content
→ comments and private messages
→ WeChat follow-up
→ sample brief feedback
→ optional Beijing coffee chat or small salon
→ validation memo
```

## 5. Positioning Adjustment

Better China-first positioning:

```text
我在北京做一个AI商业实验：
让AI每天追踪新变化，筛选可能能赚钱的机会，
再用真实反馈验证它到底是不是纸上谈兵。
```

Avoid:

- guaranteed income
- investment advice
- exaggerated automation claims
- "AI替你赚钱" style messaging
- copying English SaaS positioning without local proof
- relying only on overseas AI product references

## 6. Trust Strategy

In this context, trust may come from:

- transparent experiment logs
- showing real artifacts
- admitting uncertainty
- documenting failures
- explaining methodology
- being reachable via WeChat after interest appears
- optional offline conversation

Trust may also come from choosing a modest first offer:

```text
先帮你拆一个AI机会方向，
看它是否值得验证。
```

This is likely easier to sell early than a full subscription product.
